# 003 — Peter Naur

**Cluster:** C1 — Foundations of intent capture & specification
**Roster role:** ALGOL 60 report editor (BNF co-creator); theory-building theory of software
**SpecForge anchors:** README.md thesis ("intent is trapped in prose"), vision/north-star.md

## Why this engineer
Naur edited the ALGOL 60 report — the model of a language defined precisely enough that independent tools cannot disagree — and then wrote the paper SpecForge's thesis compresses: "Programming as Theory Building" holds that a program is a theory in the minds of its builders, and that text alone cannot carry it. That is exactly why agents reading prose guess, hallucinate, and rework. SpecForge answers Naur by externalizing the theory into a validated, typed graph the team and every agent share — north-star.md's "it reads the graph" is a direct reply to his challenge.

## References for SpecForge
**Key works**
- [Programming as Theory Building](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0165607485900328) — *Microprocessing and Microprogramming* 15(5), 1985. SpecForge's thesis in one paper: the real artifact is the shared theory; documents that don't re-create it are dead text — a spec graph is an attempt to make the theory inspectable.
- **Report on the Algorithmic Language ALGOL 60** (ed.) — *Communications of the ACM* 3(5), 1960. Proof that a prose-free, exactly-specified shared artifact lets many independent implementations agree — the precedent for multiple compilers emitting one Graph Protocol.
- *Computing: A Human Activity* — Addison-Wesley/ACM Press, 1992. Naur's collected essays, including the extended treatment of theory building and mental models; the wider frame for why docs drift from intent.
